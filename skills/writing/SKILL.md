# Writing

Write for the reader's actual decision or task. State what happened, what it means, and what remains uncertain without trying to make ordinary facts sound important.

## Start with the material

Identify the audience, purpose, required format, and source material before drafting. Read the relevant code, document, data, or cited source rather than filling gaps with plausible language.

Separate facts, informed interpretation, and opinion. Name sources when attribution matters. When something is unknown, say so plainly. Do not manufacture detail, certainty, quotes, or consensus.

## Write directly

Use concrete nouns, active or plain verbs, and the amount of structure the material needs. Let sentence and paragraph length follow the ideas. Prefer an exact fact, example, consequence, or trade-off to a claim that something is important, robust, seamless, or significant.

End a sentence when it has made its point. Cut trailing commentary that repeats, inflates, or explains how the reader should feel. Use headings, lists, and tables only when they make the information easier to find or compare.

Keep terminology consistent when precision matters. Do not force synonym variation that obscures the subject. Match the established voice and style guide when one exists.

## Edit for signal

On a final pass, remove generic introductions, ceremonial conclusions, empty transitions, decorative adjectives, stock contrasts, and summaries that repeat the preceding text. Replace vague attribution with the named source or a direct statement of uncertainty.

Check that every recommendation says who should do what, under which condition, and why. Keep the result as short as the reader's job allows.

## Verify

Confirm facts, figures, dates, links, names, commands, and code references against the source material. Preserve the requested format and any repository-specific documentation conventions. Ask a focused question only when the missing audience, purpose, or source would materially change the result.

## Boundaries

- Do not turn writing into marketing language unless that is the requested genre.
- Do not add citations, claims, or commitments that the evidence does not support.
- Do not use a prose preference to remove necessary technical precision, warnings, or qualifications.
